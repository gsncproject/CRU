When Samuel Barnes tells you that he wants to meet with you, you better go or that man will stand at the foot of your bed at three in the morning asking for your play script. That is one of the reasons why I am scared of him. Is anyone else scared of their own producer? I mean this guy has enough pressure to fire me and ruin my life professionally, both within an hour. 

Now to the thing I don‘t get: He wants to meet in Hyde Park. Out of all places he picks the most crowded in London? But yes, I am now standing in Hyde Park, desperately trying to find my biggest nightmare.

„Jeremy!“ someone behind me shouts, I turn around— Oh God, help me. It‘s Samuel. And I still hate this grey trechcoat he always wears, it just doesn‘t go with his already grey hair… I know people who would call this a fashion crime.

I smile uncertain, „Hello, Samuel…“

He points a finger at me, while still advancing, „Tell me you have a finished story.“

A story? Yes. Unwanted trauma? Yes. Finished? Not quite. „Working on it.“

He snaps his finger in my face and I flinch back, „‚Working on it‘ is not good enough! I need it yesterday!“

Can I strangle him? Or pull a Sunset Boulevard on him? „I‘ve been busy, Sam—“

„Busy my arse!“ His finger now almost touches my nose, „You will deliever or I will drop you, Jeremy!“

I gently push his finger out of my face, „Personal space is still a thing, you know?“

„I don‘t certainly care about your bloody personal space!“ he yells in my face. I really hope he drops me, I don‘t think he particulary likes me. Dito, I don‘t like him either. He‘s scary. He‘s the nightmare that makes you crawl in mum and dad‘s bed in the middle of the night. „What‘s your premise?“ he asks suddenly.

My brain malfunctions for a moment, „Premise?“

Samuel jabs his finger at my chest, „Your plot, twat!“

„Oh,“ my brain still malfunctions, I glance around me as if the bypassers could offer me help, but they don‘t seem to care that a man gets verbally abused by his producer, „My premise…“ I repeat, pretending I am thinking, „Well… i-it‘s a kid that hallucinates this dead outlaw and it ends with the kid dead.“

Sam stares at me for a moment and I think he is considering it, but his expression tells me nothing. „Dead?“ he repeats, „HAVE YOU LOST YOUR BLOODY MIND?“

I visibly flinch. Some bypassers pay us a disgusted glare, and I deeply believe they think Samuel is my father. If he was my dad I would‘ve jumped off a bridge already. Talking of my father, he wasn‘t better than Sam. He also yelled a lot in my face. I remember when I came home with a D, that was an experience I won‘t forget—

„YOU CAN‘T KILL A KID ON STAGE!“ Samuel continues, „There are ethicals, JEREMY!“

Again, Sunset Boulevard and Blasted exist… „But Sunset—“

His finger jabs at me once more, „I DON‘T CARE ABOUT FUCKING BROADWAY!“ I instinctively take a step back. He still invades my personal space. Rude.

„I‘m just saying,“ I start slowly, „I deeply believe that the West End deserves something murder-y adjecent… I mean, there is Heathers.“ That part is true, in Heathers people also die, minors even.

Samuel steps closer to me once more, waaaayyy to close by the way, „I. Don‘t. Care. About. Fucking. BROADWAY!“ Now he stares into my soul. Help. Someone please help.

I take another step back, „I promise you it‘ll be good.“ It better be good, because anything else and I will be jobless. Possibly homeless, too. Or I just go couching at Korra‘s. But Loenna will murder me if I do it again.
