**Characters in this scene**: [[Jeremy Cade]], [[Clyde Barrow (CRU)]]

The theater looks exactly like every night. Almost as if it doesn‘t care that I just got rejected by my crush without being verbally rejected.

I hate love.

This time I sit on the edge of the stage, not in the audience. My legs just hang over the edge while I‘m twisting my fingers so hard it hurts.

„Writer,“ that familiar drawl starts, „Ain‘t lookin‘ good today.“

„Whatever,“ I mutter, still twisting my fingers as he sat down next to me. I always forget he smells like dust and smoke, which is gross.

He leans back on his hands, „A rat nibble yer book?“

I shake my head, „Nah, my best friend who‘s also my crush, is dating someone else.“

„Damn,“ he chuckles, “Love‘s a funny thing, ain‘t it?“

My fingers twist harder, „Yeah, you can say it like its easy. You got Bonnie—“

„Hold yer horses, Writer,“ he drawls, „I ain‘t the real Clyde Barrow, you know?“

I glance at him and gesture vaguely, „But you sure look like him.“

„Called shapeshifin‘.“

At this point I don‘t care if he is Clyde Barrow or Uncle Gary.  He‘s weird, and always drawls in this horrible Texan accent. 

I shrug, „She chose someone else,“ which still hurts at lot, because I doubt that this Finn knows her as good as I do.

„Yep.“

Yep? That‘s all he‘s going to say? YeP. „That‘s it?“ I look at him in disgust, „No… Have you never heard of empathy?“

He shrugs, meeting my glance, „Ain‘t my story, Writer.“

„Sure,“ I cross my arms across my chest, „A story that makes no sense.“

Clyde leans closer, „Sure it does. Ya change the story, easy as that, Writer.“

Can he for once stop calling me writer? I‘m not a writer, I‘m a playwright. That‘s entirely different.

He places a hand on my shoulder. Help, the ghost Texan touches me, „Life‘s a gamble, ya know?“
