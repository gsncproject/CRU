**Characters in this scene**: [[Jeremy Cade]], Loenna Thornton, Loenna‘s classmates

Back when I graduated High School I was happy that I did, because I never planned to go back. Now the little sister of my best friend drags me to her own school. 

From what Loenna told me already, hers is worse than any High School in the UK and USA combined. Glad, that she only takes me to theater rehearsal. You may ask why… I don‘t know. And honestly, I don‘t want to know.

We walk down the empty hallway, here and there is a locker thingy, but that is it. My notebook sits tight in my left hand, specifically out of Missus-I-critize-playwrights reach.

Suddenly, she looks at me and goes, „You know, you should really change that ‘Detective Richard Thornton‘, he‘s a dick.“

Deep breath in, slowly exhale and you‘ll be fine. Hopefully. „No,“ I say, „Name fits. Conversation over.“

I hear her sigh in that annoying way teens do when they don‘t get what they want. I‘m certainly not giving her what she wants, this is my play and not hers. Who does she think she is? My mum? And mum is dead. God, rest her soul. Mine too.

„Then you should make Thornton corrupt,“ Loenna shrugs, „You need one villain anyway—„

I wave my notebook, „I have a villain and he is the devils reincarnation of Clyde Chestnut Barrow. I thought you read this!“

She crosses her arms, „He‘s a ghost.“

„An entity.“

Her face starts making this weird thing… something between ‘I will hit you‘ and ‘I will keep arguing until you admit I‘m right‘. Teenagers are scary beings. Like tiny-not-so-tiny gremlins with an attitute.

We seem to reach the theater hall, or whatever because now Loenna pushes a door open. I follow her and stop immediately in the doorway. First of all, since when do schools have such grand theater halls? Secondly, more teens. Someone please murder me right here and now.

Gremlins were scattered all through the room. In the seats. On the stage. One laid on the floor like he‘s been stabbed, lucky you. Can someone stab me too?

„That‘s Jeremy Cade!“ A girl screams.

Nope, I‘m gone. That‘s my sign to turn around and leave and run for my playwright life. I start turning around when Loenna grabs my shoulders and pushes me down the stairs towards the stage, „Yeah, he‘ll watch our rehearsal today,“ she grins.

„I will what?“ I manage, but it comes out to high. Damn, I didn‘t know I could put my voice that high.

Loenna keeps pushing me, while this hoard of gremlins stares at us, „You need inspo, right?“ 

„I need alcohol— No, better drugs!“ I deadpan.

„So, watch,“ she forces me down in one of the front row seats, „Stay there.“

I glance behind me, back towards the exit. God, that exit sign glowed like it was calling me, „But—“

Loenna rips my notebook out of my hand, „Nope, watch.“

My eyes find her again. I try reaching for my notebook but she holds it out of reach. Betrayal. I was betrayed. There is no way I can watch the theater rehearsal of some year 12. I‘m gonna die. Kids cannot act. Someone kill me. Preferrable with a gun, please and hit my head.
